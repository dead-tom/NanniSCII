# Artist statement

For this project, I drew on the US Consumer Financial Protection Bureau's Consumer Complaint Database, a public archive of millions of grievances against banks, lenders and credit providers, each one a modern descendant of Nanni's tablet. Only narratives shared with the consent of their authors are published, and every personal detail has been scrubbed from the record, reduced to a string of anonymous "XXXX", so that what remains is not the person but the pattern of their frustration. Selecting a single line from each complaint, I used CuneiScribe, an experimental machine-translation library, to translate the English into Akkadian and convert it into Unicode cuneiform signs, before rendering those signs as images and translating them once more into ASCII art, the alphabet through which machines first learned to read us. The translations are approximations made by a machine, not a scholar: Akkadian has no word for an overdraft fee or a credit score, and so the ancient language carries the exasperation while the modern details fall away. In this sense the work enacts the argument of the essay itself. These are human voices passed through the archive, read and rewritten by an artificial intelligence, and returned to us in a form that is recognisably ours, and yet no longer quite our own.
